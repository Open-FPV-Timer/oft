OFT bench bring-up

First hardware test of the single-pilot gate timer: a Seeed XIAO ESP32-C5 (the Node) and a Waveshare RP2350-POE-ETH (the Timer). Work top to bottom from a Windows PC in Chrome or Edge. Nothing here has run on real hardware yet, so expect to find problems; each step says what to note down if it fails.

Before you start

  • Use Chrome or Edge. The flasher talks to the board through Web Serial, which Firefox on Windows does not have by default.
  • This page is served with a self-signed certificate. You already clicked through the warning; the browser will show "Not secure" in the address bar, which is expected.
  • You need: both boards, a USB-C data cable, the 5.8 GHz antenna, an Ethernet cable, and a video transmitter (a quad is fine) on a known channel.
  • The Timer needs to be on the same network as the server this page comes from, so it can be found and shown above.
Wiring sheet (PDF)
1

Node on its own: flash and look at the signal

No soldering needed. This proves the XIAO, its radio and the antenna before anything is wired.

  1. Plug the antenna onto the XIAO's u.FL socket. It presses straight down and clicks.
  2. Plug the XIAO into the PC with USB-C. Windows should add a COM port by itself ("USB JTAG/serial debug unit").
  3. Open the Node page (button below, new tab). Click Flash device, choose the preset Seeed Studio XIAO C5, pick the board's port, and flash. The version shown should start with oft-.
  4. Click Connect (top right) and pick the same port. You should see a live spectrum.
  5. Power the video transmitter a metre or two away. A peak should appear on its channel and drop when you unplug it.
Open the Node page
Good: flashing ends with "Done", the spectrum updates, and the transmitter's peak is clearly above the floor.
If the board is not offered or flashing stalls: put it in download mode by hand. Hold the B (boot) button, tap R (reset), release B, then flash again. Brand-new C5 boards can hang after a USB reset until our firmware is on them. If it still fails, note the exact message in the dialog.
Do not flash from the public widscan58 page. It installs the upstream firmware, which cannot talk to a Timer.
2

Timer on its own: flash and find it

Also no soldering. This proves the RP2350 board and its Ethernet.

  1. Download the firmware file (button below).
  2. Hold the BOOT button on the RP2350 board, plug in its USB-C, then release. A drive named RP2350 appears in Explorer.
  3. Copy oft-timer.uf2 onto that drive. The drive disappears by itself; that is the board restarting into the firmware.
  4. Leave the USB-C cable plugged in. Next time, the Update Timer firmware… button in the Timer panel puts the board into this mode without the BOOT button.
  5. Plug in Ethernet. Power can stay on USB-C for now, or come from a PoE port once the PoE module is fitted.
  6. Wait about ten seconds, then click Find Timer on the network at the top of this page.
Download oft-timer.uf2
Good: a Timer named oft-xxxx with host rp2350 is found, and the panel shows Node: not connected. That is correct, nothing is wired yet.
If nothing is found: check the link lights on the Ethernet jack, then look in your router's DHCP list for a device whose MAC address starts with 02:4F:46:54 and type its IP into the panel. If it has no lease at all, the Ethernet chip is probably not starting; that is the least-tested part of the firmware, so tell me and we will look at it together.
3

Solder

Only once steps 1 and 2 pass, so a dead board can still go back unmodified.

  1. XIAO back pads first, before its headers. Solder a thin flexible wire (30 AWG or similar, 5 to 8 cm) to MTMS. If you want them for later, also EN and BOOT. Page 2 of the wiring sheet shows which pad is which.
  2. Run those wires out sideways, add a dab of hot glue or Kapton over the joints, and end each in a single male pin.
  3. XIAO headers: seat the header strips in the breadboard, drop the board on, solder.
  4. RP2350 headers: check how the PoE module mounts before choosing which way the pins face, then solder the same way.
Park unused EN and BOOT wires each in their own empty breadboard row. EN touching ground resets the Node; BOOT held low at power-up puts it in download mode.

Afterwards, repeat the quick checks: the XIAO still connects on the Node page, and the Timer is still found.

4

Four wires: the Node link

With everything unpowered, wire these on the breadboard. Go by the labels printed on the boards.

  1. Unplug the XIAO's USB cable. The Node now gets power through the red wire.
  2. Power the RP2350 board (USB-C or PoE) with Ethernet connected.
  3. Watch the Timer panel at the top. Within a few seconds it should show Node: connected, a sample rate near 1000 Hz, and a signal value.
  4. Set Seat 0 frequency to your transmitter's channel and click Set.
  5. Power the transmitter and move it toward the antenna and away again, taking a second or so. The bar should rise past the red line and a crossing should appear in the list.
Good: Node connected, clock "ping" and synced, signal rises with the transmitter, one crossing per pass.
Never have the XIAO's USB and PoE connected at the same time while the red wire is in. The XIAO's 5V pin is wired straight to its USB socket, so the two supplies would be joined. For the same reason, do not leave one board powered and the other not.
If the Node stays "not connected": the usual cause is the two data wires swapped, so swap orange and blue at one end and try again. Next check that the XIAO has power (its LED) and shares ground. If it connects but the signal never moves, check the frequency and the antenna.
5

Fifth wire: SYNC

SYNC is what gives the Timer precise lap times. Without it the clock is fitted from pings, which works but is coarser.

  1. Power off. Add the purple wire from RP2350 6 (pin 9) to the XIAO's MTMS pad wire.
  2. Power on and watch Clock in the panel.
Good: Clock changes from "ping" to sync, and the error figure settles to a small number of microseconds.
If it stays on "ping": the Node is not seeing the pulses. Check the joint on the MTMS pad and that the wire is on pin 9, not a neighbour. Note what the panel shows.
6

A few timed passes

  1. Click clear list, then make ten passes at a steady pace, at least three seconds apart (closer passes are ignored on purpose: the minimum lap time is 2 s).
  2. Check you get exactly ten crossings, and that "Since previous" looks like your pace.
  3. Try one slow pass and one fast pass, and one at longer range.

Worth writing down for me afterwards: missed or doubled passes, any flags in the list (clipped, low_snr, truncated), the floor and peak values, and the dropped count.

Known rough edge: the very first crossing after power-up can be placed up to about 0.1 s late while the noise floor settles. Ignore the first pass when judging timing.

Not part of this test

  • Reset and boot wires (RP2350 7 and 8 to EN and BOOT). The Timer cannot flash the Node yet, so they do nothing useful today.
  • Finding the Timer automatically. The RP2350 firmware does not advertise itself yet; the Find button scans the network instead.
  • Settings surviving a power cycle. The frequency goes back to 5800 MHz after every restart of the Timer.
